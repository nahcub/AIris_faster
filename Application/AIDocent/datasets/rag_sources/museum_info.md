# Museum & Gallery Info

## General Visitor Information

Northlight Museum is open Tuesday through Sunday, 10:00–18:00 (last entry 17:15), and
closed Mondays. The exhibition *Devotion and Likeness: Painting North of the Alps,
1450–1550* occupies three floors of the North Wing. Free audio guides are available at
the entrance desk in English and Korean. The museum café is on the ground floor, next
to the main lobby; the gift shop is adjacent to the exhibition exit on Floor 3. Photography
without flash is permitted throughout the exhibition. Guided docent tours in Korean run
at 11:00 and 14:00 daily; English tours run at 13:00.

## Floor Map Overview

- **Floor 1 (mezzanine) — Dutch Renaissance Room**: works by the three Dutch-school
  painters in the exhibition, all active 1520–1550, associated with the "Romanism"
  style (Italian-influenced Dutch painting).
- **Floor 2 — German Wing**, two rooms:
  - **Room 2A, Early German Panel Painting**: mid-to-late 15th century German works.
  - **Room 2B, German Renaissance Masters**: German works from 1500–1550, including
    Albrecht Dürer, the Cranach workshop, and Hans Holbein the Younger.
- **Floor 3 — Flemish Wing**, two rooms:
  - **Room 3A, Early Flemish Masters**: mid-to-late 15th century Flemish/Early
    Netherlandish works.
  - **Room 3B, Flemish Renaissance**: Flemish works from 1490–1535, showing growing
    Italian influence.

Darmstadt Madonna (Hans Holbein the Younger) is on **Floor 2, Room 2B**.

## Exhibition Composition (for "what kind of show is this" questions)

The exhibition leans heavily religious: about 65% of the 295 works are religious
subjects (Madonnas, Crucifixions, altarpiece panels), 22% are portraits, and the
remainder is split among mythological, landscape, and historical scenes. By school,
Flemish works are the largest group (137), followed by German (124) and Dutch (34).
This reflects the real composition of the curated set, not an invented statistic.
